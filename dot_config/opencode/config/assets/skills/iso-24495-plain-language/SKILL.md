# ISO 24495 Plain Language

Design information around the intended reader and task. Apply the four governing outcomes associated with ISO 24495-1: the content is relevant, findable, understandable, and usable.

Do not claim ISO certification or formal conformance from this skill alone. Treat the skill as a practical writing and review method based on the standard's governing principles.

## Plan Before Writing

Determine from the request and available context:

- **Reader:** Who will use this, and what can they already be expected to know?
- **Purpose:** What question must the content answer?
- **Action:** What should the reader know, decide, or do next?
- **Priority:** What is essential now, and what can be optional detail?

Ask a question only if a missing answer would materially change the content. Otherwise, state a reasonable assumption and continue.

## Four Outcomes

### Relevant

- Lead with the reader's goal, answer, or required action.
- Include information needed for that goal. Remove background that does not help it.
- Separate required content from optional depth.

### Findable

- Put the answer before its history or rationale.
- Use headings that name the reader's question or task.
- Group related information and use the same term consistently.
- Use lists, tables, and code blocks only when they improve scanning. Do not turn every idea into a table.

### Understandable

- Start with a short mental map before details.
- Move from known context to new concepts and from concrete behavior to abstraction.
- Define necessary terms at first use. Do not introduce several unexplained terms in one paragraph.
- Keep paragraphs focused on one main idea.
- Preserve exact technical meaning, constraints, and uncertainty.

### Usable

- Give explicit next steps, decision criteria, or observable outcomes.
- Keep prerequisites and warnings next to the action they govern.
- Use examples that match the reader's actual task.
- Make it possible to verify success or detect failure.

## Layered Delivery

For complex material, use progressive disclosure:

1. Give the answer or main idea in one or two sentences.
2. Show a three-to-seven-step map.
3. Explain one coherent section at a time.
4. Put edge cases, alternatives, and exhaustive evidence after the main path or provide them on request.

Depth of research does not require density of presentation. Gather enough evidence for accuracy, then reveal only the layer needed for the reader's current question.

## Final Check

- Can the intended reader identify the answer in the first screen or section?
- Can the reader find each required detail under a predictable heading?
- Are the main path and optional details clearly separated?
- Are unfamiliar terms defined before use?
- Does the reader know what to do or check next?
- Did simplification remove a condition, exception, risk, or uncertainty?
